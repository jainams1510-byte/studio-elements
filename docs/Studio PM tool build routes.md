# Only a custom PWA clears every requirement

**Build the tool as a custom React PWA on Supabase, and keep Google Sheets + Apps Script with an AppSheet front end as the main alternative.** The custom route is the only one of the three that delivers all the hard requirements in a single installable phone app: offline capture of photos and Hindi/Gujarati voice notes, a true critical-path Gantt, and a read-only client view enforced by the database. It also fits the assumed budget, at roughly **₹2,500–3,150 a month before GST** (Supabase Pro at a flat USD 25, free static hosting, and under ₹800 of speech-to-text and LLM usage). No-code fails on a hard requirement in each case: Airtable has no offline mode and costs about ₹9,500 a month for five editors, while Notion has no critical path and downloads only the first 50 rows of a database for offline use. The Sheets route costs less (about ₹1,600 a month with GST for five seats) and is the quickest for registers, budgets and bills, but an Apps Script web app cannot work as an offline PWA, the critical path has to be hand-coded, and voice notes live outside the app. The price of the custom route is paid in build effort and security risk rather than rupees per month: no source measures build time for this scope, and the documented failure point of AI-built apps is access control, so a paid developer review of the data model, access policies and offline queue belongs in the plan. Whichever route is built, the same platform facts apply: iPhones upload only while the app is open, Sarvam leads independent Gujarati transcription benchmarks at ₹0.49 a minute, WhatsApp hand-off needs no API, and the main DPDP duties become enforceable in mid-May 2027.

All rupee conversions below use about ₹95 per US dollar; mid-market was 94.26 on 13 November 2026 ([CurrencyBeacon](https://currencybeacon.com/rates/USD/INR)). Figures exclude GST and card forex mark-up unless stated. The 3–5 project load, 10 or so users and ₹3,000–5,000 target are the brief's assumptions; AI volumes are the researchers' assumptions and are marked where used.

## Offline capture and critical path eliminate Airtable and Notion

The requirement list contains three items that most platforms cannot meet together: a site log that works with no signal, voice notes in Hindi and Gujarati, and a full critical-path schedule. The table shows where each route stands.

| Requirement | Airtable | Notion | Sheets + Apps Script (AppSheet front end) | Custom React PWA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offline site log on a phone | No offline mode | Partial: per-page opt-in, first 50 rows, no forms or buttons | Yes, through AppSheet's queued sync; not through an Apps Script web app | Yes, but built: local queue or sync layer |
| Photos captured offline | No | Not documented | AppSheet queues and downsizes photos; fully offline capture not explicitly documented | Custom blob queue on the device |
| Hindi/Gujarati voice notes | No audio support | Business plan, online only, neither language listed | No documented audio capture; record outside the app, transcribe by script | In-app recording plus an external speech API |
| Critical-path Gantt | Native on paid plans; basic on phones | None; found by eye | Timeline view is display-only; critical path hand-coded | Licensed Gantt component |
| Read-only client view | Free read-only access; weak field-level control | Free guests | Separate per-project file or filtered report | Database policy per table |
| Five internal users, monthly | about ₹9,500 | about ₹4,750 | about ₹1,350 | about ₹2,375 (Supabase) |

**Airtable fails the site test outright.** Its own requirements page lists an internet connection as mandatory on every app and says offline access is unsupported ([Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/articles/5016991853-airtable-technical-requirements)); a consultant confirmed in September 2026 that the mobile app cannot create records or take photos offline ([Airtable community](https://community.airtable.com/automations-8/photo-compression-offline-data-collection-in-airtable-mobile-48676)). It does have the Gantt the founder wants, with dependencies and a critical-path highlight on paid plans, though phones get only basic functionality ([Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/docs/gantt-view-milestones-dependencies-and-critical-paths)). Its AI fields read documents and images but list no audio formats ([Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/docs/using-airtable-ai-in-fields)).

**Notion fails in the opposite places.** Offline editing now exists on every plan, but each page is switched on by hand per device, a database brings down only its first 50 rows, and forms and buttons stop working ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/guides/working-offline-in-notion-everything-you-need-to-know)). Whether photos attached offline are queued for upload is undocumented, which is the most important unverified point for a photo log. Critical path is absent: Notion's own guidance is to scan the timeline for the longest chain ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/blog/project-schedule)). Its transcription feature needs the Business plan, works only online, and its language list omits Hindi and Gujarati ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/help/ai-meeting-notes)).

**The Sheets route survives only with AppSheet in front of it.** Apps Script serves web apps inside a sandboxed iframe ([Google](https://developers.google.com/apps-script/guides/html/restrictions?hl=fr)), and the service worker an offline PWA needs cannot be supplied that way according to dated community answers ([Stack Overflow archive](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/51458858/how-to-develop-progressive-web-app-with-google-app-script)); no official Google statement confirms this, so treat it as well-supported rather than proven. AppSheet fills the gap: it caches the app and data on the device and queues changes until sync ([AppSheet Help](https://support.google.com/appsheet/answer/10107724?hl=en)), and it captures and downsizes photos from the phone camera ([AppSheet Help](https://support.google.com/appsheet/answer/10106529?hl=en)). Two gaps remain. No Google documentation shows an audio-recording column, so voice notes would be recorded in another app and picked up from Drive by a script. And the built-in timeline view, available only on paid Workspace plans, shows bars without dependencies or critical path ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4094096/how-to-use-timeline-view-in-google-sheets.html)), so the forward and backward pass would be custom script.

**The custom PWA meets every row because each capability is assembled rather than inherited.** Syncfusion's React Gantt highlights tasks with zero or negative slack and recalculates as dates and dependencies change ([Syncfusion](https://help.syncfusion.com/gantt-sdk/react/gantt-chart/critical-path)); iOS has supported in-browser recording through MediaRecorder since 14.5 ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)); and the database itself can refuse a client's write. Nothing here comes for free, which is the subject of the next two sections.

## Every viable route fits ₹5,000 a month; build effort separates them

Running cost does not decide this choice among the routes that work. It only rules out Airtable.

| Route | Platform cost per month | Against ₹3,000–5,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Airtable Team, 5 editors | USD 100, about ₹9,500 | Exceeds, about double |
| Notion Plus, 5 members | USD 50, about ₹4,750 (about ₹5,600 if 18% GST is a real cost) | Upper edge |
| Workspace Business Starter, 5 seats, AppSheet Core included | ₹1,350 (₹1,593 with GST) | Well inside |
| Supabase Pro | USD 25, about ₹2,375 (about ₹2,800 with GST) | Inside |
| Firebase Blaze | about ₹0–200 (estimate from a proxy storage rate) | Well inside |
| Speech-to-text plus LLM, any route | about ₹150–770 (assumed volumes) | Add to any row |

Airtable Team lists at USD 20 per user per month on annual billing, charged for anyone with edit rights, while read-only collaborators are free ([Airtable](https://airtable.com/pricing), [Airtable](https://support.airtable.com/docs/how-collaborators-impact-billing)). Notion Plus lists at USD 10 per seat with guests free ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/pricing)). Indian reseller guides converge on **₹270 per user per month for Workspace Business Starter** with 30 GB pooled per user, though Google's own India price page could not be opened and other figures of ₹160.65 and ₹540 appear on Google pages, so confirm at checkout ([ScaleSaathi](https://scalesaathi.in/guides/google-workspace-pricing-india/)). That plan bundles AppSheet Core for each domain user ([AppSheet Help](https://support.google.com/appsheet/answer/10105400?hl=en)); whether outside clients can use an AppSheet app under those licences is unresolved.

On the custom side, **Supabase Pro costs a flat USD 25** with 8 GB of database, 100 GB of file storage and seven days of daily backups, quotas this workload does not approach; the Free plan's 1 GB of storage, lack of backups and pause after a quiet week make it a prototype tier only ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/pricing)). Firebase's database and sign-in usage fit inside its no-cost quotas, but file storage and functions now require the pay-as-you-go Blaze plan ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/pricing?authuser=8)). A static PWA hosts free for commercial use on Cloudflare Pages, whereas Vercel's free plan is limited to non-commercial use ([Flavio Copes](https://flaviocopes.com/hosting-free-tiers/), [Hostfleet](https://hostfleet.pages.dev/cf-pages-vs-vercel-vs-netlify-2026/)).

The custom route carries two one-off costs that the others avoid. **No free open-source Gantt edition includes critical path.** SVAR's PRO edition costs USD 749 for one developer, about ₹71,000 ([SVAR](https://svar.dev/react/gantt/pricing)), and DHTMLX PRO starts at USD 799 ([DHTMLX](https://dhtmlx.com/docs/products/licenses)); spread over three years, either adds about ₹2,000 a month. Syncfusion's community licence gives the full component set free to companies under USD 1 million in revenue with up to five developers ([Syncfusion](https://www.syncfusion.com/blogs/post/be-empowered-by-our-community-license.md)), which the studio appears to meet, but the full current eligibility terms were not verified.

The second cost is the build itself, and here the evidence is thin. **No source quantifies build time for a tool of this scope on any route.** Published rate cards put a mid-level Indian freelance full-stack developer at USD 20–52 an hour, from a staffing vendor with no stated sample ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/cost-to-hire/india/)). As a planning estimate only, not a finding: if a robust first version takes 300–600 hours, a fully outsourced build costs roughly ₹5.7–20 lakh, several times the fee the studio earns on one project. That makes founder-built-with-AI plus targeted paid review the only financially sensible form of this route; 40–80 hours of review would cost roughly ₹0.8–2.6 lakh on the same rates. The record of non-developers building with AI tools cuts both ways. A project manager with no coding background shipped an app in about six weeks, by his own account published by the tool's vendor ([Anthropic](https://claude.com/blog/how-a-non-technical-project-manager-built-and-shipped-a-stress-management-app-with-claude-code-in-six-weeks)); a fintech founder spent nearly four months and reached launch with half-working sign-in and no tests, in an account published by an agency that sells rebuilds ([Quokka Labs](https://dev.to/varsha_ojha_5b45cb023937b/what-happens-when-a-fintech-founder-builds-an-app-with-claude-code-and-it-falls-apart-before-launch-58ab)).

On speed, the ranking is an inference rather than a measurement. Sheets is fastest for registers, budget roll-ups and a scripted weekly PDF, because the team already works in spreadsheets, and slowest at the critical-path Gantt and offline capture. No-code is quick to start but, for this scope, tends to grow into a multi-tool stack; one construction practitioner runs Airtable with four other tools and calls it still a work in progress ([r/ConstructionTech](https://reddit.birdcat.cafe/r/ConstructionTech/comments/1k1l1es/anyone_using_airtable_coda_or_monday_in)). The custom route is slowest to a first usable version and the only one whose finish line includes every requirement.

## Data integrity and access control, not capacity, decide scalability

Capacity is a non-issue everywhere. A Sheets file now holds 20 million cells ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/09/doubled-cell-limits-in-google-sheets-now-generally-available.html)), and Apps Script's daily quotas dwarf this load; the limit that shapes design is the six-minute hard stop per execution ([Google](https://developers.google.com/apps-script/guides/services/quotas?hl=en)). What scales badly is trust in the data as projects, people and money accumulate.

**Sheets has no transactions, and its failures are silent.** A write that fails midway leaves a partial state that cannot be rolled back, and triggers stop without warning when the owning account changes ([Buralog](https://buralog.jp/en/approval-workflow-sheets-en/)). Two users who read the same value and both write back lose one update unless every write path takes a script lock ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/hayrullahkar/building-a-scalable-inventory-web-app-with-google-sheets-apps-script-42l8)). Offline edits to a raw sheet resolve same-cell clashes by keeping the latest edit ([Sheets Bootcamp](https://www.sheetsbootcamp.com/google-sheets-offline/)), which is exactly how a drawing revision code gets reverted. For a tool whose purpose includes stopping work from an old drawing and tracking certified bills, these are central weaknesses. They are manageable with append-only ledgers, script-only editing and a studio-owned automation account, but each safeguard is a convention, not a guarantee. Client separation is the same: the Sheet itself cannot hide rows, so each client needs a separate file or a Looker Studio report filtered by sign-in email, where a differently-cased address matches nothing ([Looker Studio docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/looker/docs/studio/filter-by-email-address?authuser=4)).

**The custom route moves those guarantees into the database, and moves the risk into the build.** In Supabase a row-level security policy acts as a filter Postgres applies to every query, and a table with row-level security switched on returns nothing until a policy allows it ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/auth/auth-deep-dive/auth-row-level-security)). A client with no write policy cannot write. The same page states the danger: a table left without row-level security is open to any role with a grant. This is the most common failure in AI-built apps. In a May 2025 study, 170 of 1,645 sampled apps from one AI builder exposed personal data and about 70% had row-level security switched off ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/lovable-vibe-coding-security-crisis-exposed)). Nearly all such evidence reaches us through security firms and rescue agencies with an interest in the conclusion, but it points at precisely this tool's sensitive parts: the client view and the money records.

**Supabase suits this data better than Firebase; Firebase suits offline better than Supabase.** Budget lines, RA bills, certifications and payments are relational, and Supabase stores them as ordinary Postgres that can be dumped and restored to a self-hosted copy ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/self-hosting/restore-from-platform)). Firestore's document model and rules language are proprietary, so leaving means remodelling data and rewriting rules ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/libme/supabase-vs-firebase-in-2026-the-migration-questions-nobody-answers-35h8)). In return, Firestore ships offline persistence for documents on the web, off by default and enabled in configuration, with last-write-wins on conflicts ([Firebase](https://firebase.google.com/docs/firestore/manage-data/enable-offline?hl=en)). Supabase has none built in. PowerSync adds it with a free tier that suits ten users, while its USD 49 Pro tier would push the total to about ₹7,000 ([PowerSync](https://powersync.com/pricing)). On neither stack does the database layer cover photo and audio files; those need a custom on-device queue either way. Since the stated offline need is the site log, a narrow capture queue on Supabase meets it without a sync engine.

## Five platform constraints apply whichever route is built

### iPhones upload only while the app is open

A PWA is viable for site logging on both platforms once installed to the home screen. Storage is no longer the limit: Safari and Chrome each allow one origin up to about 60% of total disk ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/?p=14403), [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)), and a vendor guide's 50 MB figure for iOS is outdated. The real gaps are on iOS. **Background Sync is unsupported in every iOS Safari version** ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/background-sync)), so a queued upload resumes only when the user reopens the app ([Workbox](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/workbox/issues/2516)). iOS shows no install prompt, and it cannot receive files shared into the app from the gallery or WhatsApp, which Android Chrome supports for installed apps ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/), [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target)). Developers also report Safari silently dropping IndexedDB writes around backgrounding ([developer log](https://gist.github.com/pesterhazy/4de96193af89a6dd5ce682ce2adff49a)), and Safari, with tracking prevention on, deletes script-written data for sites the user has not touched in seven days of browser use ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). WebKit said in 2020 that home-screen apps keep their own counter ([OSnews](https://www.osnews.com/?p=131566)), but no 2026 primary source restates it. The design consequences are firm: read each saved entry back before confirming it, show a permanent count of items not yet uploaded, sync on open, and never let the phone hold the only copy for long. Audio containers differ by iOS version, so the app should record whatever format the device reports and the server should accept both ([Build with Matija](https://www.buildwithmatija.com/blog/md/iphone-safari-mediarecorder-audio-recording-transcription)). The team's phone mix is unknown and matters: an Android-heavy team makes this low-risk.

### Sarvam leads on Gujarati at ₹0.49 a minute

An independent 2026 benchmark from IIT Madras and Josh Talks, built on unscripted phone conversations, gives the clearest picture ([Voice of India](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19151)). Lower is better.

| System | Hindi error % | Gujarati error % |
|---|---|---|
| Sarvam Audio | 5.0 | 12.8 |
| Gemini 3 Pro | 6.0 | 15.8 |
| Amazon Transcribe | 6.8 | 17.7 |
| Gemini 3 Flash | 8.3 | 22.5 |
| GPT-4o Transcribe | 33.9 | 98.2 |

**OpenAI's GPT-4o transcription models effectively fail on Gujarati**, while Sarvam bills in rupees at ₹29.40 an hour ([Sarvam](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/getting-started/pricing.md)) and advertises mid-sentence Gujarati-English switching ([Sarvam](https://www.sarvam.ai/apis/speech-to-text/gujarati)). At an assumed 130 site logs a month with two to five minutes of speech each, that is about ₹130–320 a month. Google's Chirp 3 has Gujarati only in preview and runs only in US and EU regions ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/models/chirp-3)). Two cautions: the benchmark has no code-mixed score and no construction-site noise or trade vocabulary, and newer models from Sarvam and OpenAI were not tested. A project glossary and a human correction step before the transcript becomes the log, already a design rule here, matter more than the headline numbers.

### LLM cost stays under ₹500 a month

On the researchers' assumed volumes (about 130 logs with four resized photos each, 22 weekly reports and 100 message drafts, totalling about 1.6 million input and 0.13 million output tokens a month), a small model such as Claude Haiku 5.5 at USD 0.10 and 0.50 per million tokens costs about ₹20 a month, and a mid-tier model such as Claude Sonnet 5.5 at USD 2 and 10 costs about ₹430 ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing), [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing.md), [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/pricing)). Resizing photos on the phone before upload keeps image tokens near 1,600 per photo instead of up to 4,784 at full resolution ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). **Quality on Gujarati text and site photos, which no source compared, should drive model choice, not price.** Notion's built-in AI is the expensive path by comparison, since the full set needs the Business plan at double the Plus price ([Notion](https://www.notion.com/pricing)).

### WhatsApp hand-off needs no API

The rule that a human sends every external message is met for free. A `wa.me` link with a number and prefilled text opens that person's chat with the draft in the composer ([WhatsApp](https://whatsapp.com/faq/iphone/23559013)); for a project group, the phone's share sheet passes text and files and the user picks the group ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/How_to/Share_data_between_apps)). No link form pre-selects a group. Meta's official Groups API requires an Official Business Account and caps groups at eight participants ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/groups.md)), too small for a project group. Unofficial automation libraries breach WhatsApp's terms on auto-messaging ([ConductAtlas](https://conductatlas.com/platform/whatsapp/whatsapp-terms-of-service/provision/CA-P-030669/prohibition-on-bulk-and-auto-messaging/)) and put at risk the number on which all client coordination runs.

### DPDP duties become enforceable in mid-May 2027

The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with a phased start. The duties that bind an ordinary firm (notice, consent, security safeguards, breach reporting, erasure) take effect 18 months later, on 13 or 14 May 2027 depending on the source ([Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/), [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/data-protection-and-data-privacy/dpdp-act-rules-2026-compliance-deadline/)). **Applicability turns on digital processing of personal data, not firm size**, and penalty ceilings reach ₹250 crore for security failures ([StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/dpdp-act-timeline-phases-2026-2027-explained/)). The studio would be the responsible party for client contacts, site photos of homes and budget data, with the host and AI vendors as its processors. The practical minimum is a plain-language notice with recorded consent at onboarding, role-based access, backups, access logs kept a year, a breach procedure with a 72-hour detailed report, and deletion at project close ([Hogan Lovells](https://ca.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023-brought-into-force-), [Shardul Amarchand Mangaldas](https://www.amsshardul.com/insight/enforcement-of-the-dpdp-act-and-notification-of-the-dpdp-rules/)). Cross-border transfer is permitted unless the government restricts it, so in-India storage is prudent rather than established as mandatory; Supabase offers a Mumbai region that must be chosen explicitly at project creation ([Supabase](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/platform/regions)). This is planning guidance, not legal advice.

## Recommended stack, and what would flip the choice

The recommended build is a React PWA hosted on Cloudflare Pages, with Supabase Pro in Mumbai for database, sign-in and file storage, a hand-written offline queue limited to the site log, Syncfusion's Gantt if the community licence applies and SVAR PRO or DHTMLX PRO if not, Sarvam for transcription, and a small LLM for log tidy-up with a mid-tier one for the Friday report.

| Line | Monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Supabase Pro | about ₹2,375 |
| Static hosting | ₹0 |
| Transcription (assumed volume) | about ₹130–320 |
| LLM (assumed volume) | about ₹20–450 |
| **Total before GST** | **about ₹2,500–3,150** |
| With a paid Gantt licence spread over three years | about ₹4,500–5,150 |

Not included, because not researched: the builder's AI coding subscription, a domain, and card forex mark-up. Firebase would cut the platform line to near zero and give document offline support out of the box; choose it instead if offline use must extend beyond the site log to the whole app, accepting a harder data model for bills and budgets and a harder exit.

The route carries three conditions. First, pay a developer for the fixed set of high-risk items: data model and migrations, access policies with tests, the offline queue, a backup-and-restore drill, and a security check before any client login is issued. Second, run it internally on two or three live projects before clients see it, starting with registers and the site log, and switch on client logins and money modules only after the access rules pass their tests. Third, export the database and file bucket to the existing shared drive on a schedule.

**Choose Sheets + Apps Script with AppSheet instead if nobody can own code.** It costs about ₹1,000 a month less, builds fastest, and handles the drawing register, budget, RA bills, invoices and weekly report well. The price is a hand-coded critical path with no dependency arrows, voice notes recorded outside the app, client separation by convention, and long-term dependence on whoever wrote the scripts. It is also the natural pilot if the studio already holds a Workspace domain, which the research did not establish.

## Conclusion

The comparison the brief framed as cost, speed, scalability and offline collapses into a single question: who carries the risk. No-code platforms carry it for you but refuse two of the requirements. Sheets lets the team move fast but leaves integrity to discipline. The custom route satisfies everything on paper and hands the risk to the builder, concentrated in three places: the offline media queue, the critical-path schedule, and per-project access control. Those three are also where the paid Gantt component, the narrow queue design and the developer review each buy the risk down, which is why the recommendation holds only as a package.

The cheapest mistake to avoid is treating monthly cost as the constraint. Every workable route fits the budget with room to spare, and AI usage is a rounding error. The scarce resources are build hours and verified security. Two facts the research could not settle would sharpen the decision quickly: the team's iPhone-to-Android mix, and whether a Workspace domain already exists.
